# Connections — Example Boards

These boards are an authoring starting point for the text-only Connections game described in
[`CONNECTIONS_SPEC.md`](./CONNECTIONS_SPEC.md). They are not seed data and should not be published
without a final canon, localization, spoiler, and ambiguity review.

Each board contains exactly 16 unique tiles and four intended groups of four. The board tables are
shown in one possible shuffled order; the game should still shuffle tiles for each attempt.

## Authoring legend

| Color | Intended difficulty | Authoring goal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yellow | Straightforward | A recognizable group that gives the player a foothold |
| Green | Moderate | Familiar facts with at least one plausible distraction |
| Blue | Hard | More specific knowledge or relationships |
| Purple | Trickiest | Wordplay, titles, translations, or a strong red herring |

The colors describe editorial difficulty only. They do not affect scoring or game rules.

## Review checklist for turning an example into a Daily

- Verify every fact against the aired anime and the English localization used by the site.
- Confirm the spoiler note covers the latest tile, explanation, and category reveal.
- Put all 16 tiles on one shuffled board and search for alternate groups of four.
- Ask at least one reviewer who did not author the puzzle to solve it without hints.
- Tighten labels that rely on words such as “related,” “associated,” or “connected.”
- Check accents and spelling, especially `Lügner`, `Übel`, and `Äußerst`.
- Preview the longest tiles on a narrow mobile viewport.
- Enter the board through `/admin/connections`; do not turn this document into application seed
  data.

## Further board themes

- Four first-stage exam teams, represented by one tile per member plus an event or opponent.
- Spells grouped by caster, effect, or the episode in which they first appear.
- Locations grouped by region or by the party that visited them.
- Episode titles grouped through exact shared words rather than plot trivia.
- Character relationships such as mentor chains, siblings, party membership, or shared opponents.
- “Who am I?” paraphrases grouped by the character they describe, avoiding copyrighted dialogue.
- A board made entirely from mundane folk magic for a lighter themed day.
- Character-name translations after native-speaker and localization review.
